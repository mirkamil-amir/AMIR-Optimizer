# Quantitative audit, 2026-10-28

Scope: every quantitative calculation of MatrixView (backend, ML, frontend teaching examples), traced from the request
through the services to the stored and displayed numbers, checked against independent definitions; plus a targeted
review of the processes that feed them (market data, FX, jobs, persistence). Baseline: branch `main` + `191d2de`
(clean working tree), unit tests 365 passed / 9 skipped (live smoke), ML tests 41 passed.

Canonical results live in [formula-reference.md](formula-reference.md) (one entry per formula, status per entry); this
report records the method, the findings and what remains open. Subsystems: [subsystem-inventory.md](subsystem-inventory.md).

## Method

1. Inventory: every file under the quantitative paths of `scripts/quant/manifest.json` (48 files) read and mapped to a
   formula entry or declared formula-free (4 files, with reasons). `maintain.py validate` now enforces this mapping.
2. Independent reconstruction: each formula was re-derived from its published definition (references in the entries),
   then compared with the code, the input conventions (units, frequency, currency, dates) and the callers.
3. Evidence: hand-computed values and independent re-implementations in
   `tests/PortfolioOptimizer.UnitTests/Optimization/IndependentFormulaTests.cs` (Ledoit–Wolf with the paper's normalized
   norm, Euler risk allocation by numerical gradient, type-7 quantile, TE/IR, daily risk-free conversion, max Sharpe with
   cash against a dense search, monthly-return chaining). These tests passed on the code **before** the fixes – they
   confirm existing formulas, they are not regression tests for the defects.
4. Defects: fixed at the layer where they arise, each with a test that fails on the old code (verified by stashing the
   fix and running the tests: 5 failed before, all pass after – see [Evidence](#evidence)).

## Classification

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified correct | matches the definition; independent or hand-computed test exists |
| Correct under documented assumptions | correct given a stated modelling choice or limitation (stated in the entry) |
| Defect confirmed – fixed / – open | wrong result demonstrated; fixed with a regression test, or still open |
| Potential defect requiring more evidence | plausible wrong result, not demonstrated or impact not measured |
| Undocumented convention | a choice the docs did not state (all found ones are now documented) |
| Insufficient data or tests to establish correctness | could not be checked |
| Not applicable / Not implemented | category does not exist in the product |

Counts measured with `python3 scripts/quant/maintain.py audit` on 2026-10-28 (65 entries):

|---|---|

"Verified correct" means checked against the definition with a test; it is a statement about the formula as coded,
not about the suitability of historical estimates for investment decisions.

Severity: **High** = can silently change backtest/optimization results that users compare; **Medium** = wrong in
specific, realistic configurations; **Low** = edge case or fails safe.

## Findings

### Q-01 · Backtest execution on a day when one exchange is closed (High, fixed)

*Where:* `PortfolioSimulator.Run` (F-BT-002) – used by every backtest, every strategy comparison and the ML walk-forward
backtest. *Cause:* the calendar is the union of all stocks' trading days. On the execution day, an asset without a price
had its target set to 0, so a **held** position was sold at the previous close and its target value stayed in cash
until the next rebalance (a month). 1 May (European exchanges closed, US open), Good Friday/Easter Monday differences,
US Thanksgiving and Asian holidays hit monthly rebalances every year. *Consequence:* multi-market strategies were
systematically under-invested; results depended on holiday coincidences rather than on the strategy.
*Fix:* the rebalance waits up to 5 trading days for every involved asset to trade; after that closed positions keep
their units and the others are scaled to fit the remaining value (cash never negative); assets without a price for more
than 10 days count as no longer trading (sold at the last price, as before).
*Before/after* (deterministic fixture: EU +0.2 %/day, US +0.05 %/day, 50/50 monthly, EU closed 1 May 2024):

| | Old code | New code |
|---|---|---|
| Execution of the 30 Apr signal | 1 May, EU weight 0, invested 50 % | 2 May, EU weight 50 %, invested 100 % |
| Value on 31 May 2024 | 111,724.26 | 114,393.73 |
| Final value (28 Jun 2024) | 114,389.97 | 117,298.49 |

These are expected mathematical results on synthetic prices, not observed application results on market data.
*Effect on stored data:* backtests and comparisons are stored with their results, so existing records keep the old
numbers; re-running them gives the new ones. Single-market backtests change only where a stock has a gap in its own
price history.

### Q-01b · Signal date on which one exchange is closed (Medium, fixed)

*Where:* `BacktestEngine.Signal` (F-BT-001). An asset had to trade **on** the signal date; on a month-end holiday of its
exchange it was left out of the optimization (and then sold). Now the latest close ≤ s is used unless it is more than
10 days old (no look-ahead: only data dated ≤ s). Test: `ExchangeCalendarTests.SignalOnAHoliday_KeepsTheClosedMarketInTheOptimization`
(with the asset left out, the 80 % weight cap of the fixture was infeasible).

### Q-02 · Turnover check of the independent validator disagreed with the optimizer (Low – fails safe, fixed)

*Where:* `AllocationValidator.Validate` vs `MeanVarianceOptimizer` (F-OPT-010). With a turnover limit and a portfolio whose
holdings are all outside the chosen universe, the current weights are all zero: the optimizer (by design) ignores the
limit, the validator applied it and rejected every allocation ("failed independent validation"). Now both apply the
limit only when an asset of the universe is held; the optimizer's diagnostic says "the portfolio holds none of these
assets". Test: `AllocationValidatorTests.TurnoverLimit_WithoutHoldingsInTheUniverse_IsNotApplied_LikeTheOptimizer`.

### Verified without change (selection)

Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage (exact agreement with the paper to 1e-12), maximum-Sharpe homogenization including the cash
reserve, greedy max-return LP, bisection for the volatility limit, Euler risk contributions, historical VaR/ES, Sortino,
TE/IR, beta, CAGR vs arithmetic annualization, FX direction/inverse/cross identities and the dated carry-forward
policy, time-weighted return with end-of-day flows, trade sizing with minimum fees, weighted-average cost, ML purging/
embargo, OOS R² (Campbell–Thompson), AUC (Mann–Whitney), IC t-statistic on non-overlapping dates, ridge normal
equations and penalized logistic Newton steps. Details per entry in the reference.

## Not audited (and why)

* Language-model research workflow (`Application/Research/*`): it produces no numbers by design; its numeric outputs come
  from `OptimizationService.EvaluateAsync` / `RealityCheckService` (audited). Its rule parsers were not re-verified.
* News relevance scoring (`NewsRules`): rule-based categories, not a financial formula (tests exist, not re-derived).
* Integration and end-to-end suites were not run in this change: they need PostgreSQL (Podman) and a disposable stack;
  commands in [testing](../testing.md).
